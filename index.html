Fair odds that the athlete in the row beats the athlete in the column, given both start. Books void a matchup if either withdraws, so this conditioning is what their rules actually settle on. Back a side only where the book is longer than the price shown.

This is the market the model is actually good at. Scored over 24,126 pairs across seven championships, it beats a season-best ranking on Brier by 0.0057 (95% bootstrap 0.0011–0.0098) — an edge it does not have when picking outright winners. Probabilities near 50/50 are honest: quoted 0.552, realised 0.560. But treat lopsided pairs with care even after recalibration — the model still runs slightly hot at the top of the range, and a short matchup price is where that costs you.

Shading tracks how one-sided the matchup is. Small figure below each price is the win probability.

Ranked by predicted finishing position, not by win probability — they are not the same order, and they disagree in the top four of 28 of the 44 events. Avg pos is the athlete's mean finishing position across 50,000 simulated finals. A volatile athlete can hold the better win chance while finishing worse on average, because the tail that wins races is the same tail that produces sixth places.

Fair odds, not offered odds. These are the model's own prices. Back a runner only where a book is offering longer than the Fair column, and de-vig the book's full market before you compare — athletics outrights routinely carry a 130%+ overround.

Fields are the official entry lists — every entrant on the published start list is priced. An event marked Field NOT SET still has athletes to run their round: results post heat by heat, so until everyone entered has appeared nobody can be eliminated, and the field can only shrink from there. Those prices are indicative.

Where the model is wrong, and which way. Measured over 236 backtested finals, it underrates favourites and overrates longshots, with the crossover near a fair price of 3.0. Slots priced 1.0–3.0 win 1.19× as often as quoted (95% CI 1.11–1.30); slots priced 3.0–25 only 0.81× (0.73–0.91). Prices below are corrected for this, but the correction is the size the data supports, not necessarily the whole error — so treat apparent value at long odds with more suspicion than apparent value at short ones.

Both seasons count. Ratings blend this season with last, weighting the prior less as current-season evidence accumulates. An athlete tagged % prior season is rated mostly on last year; one tagged no race this season has not competed in the event this year and is rated below last season's level with a wider distribution. Judge those prices accordingly.

Backtested over 249 finals from seven championships: winner log loss 1.634 against a 2.468 uniform baseline, podium Brier 0.136 against 0.190, favourites hitting 51%. Strongest in hurdles and horizontal jumps, weakest in the steeplechase, combined events and marathons, where the favourite lands barely more often than chance. The two race walks rest on a single undated entry-list mark each — the results feed carries no walk data at all — and are flagged accordingly. Nothing here is a guarantee — stake only what you can afford to lose.
