Fair odds that the athlete in the row beats the athlete in the column, given both start. Books void a matchup if either withdraws, so this conditioning is what their rules actually settle on. Back a side only where the book is longer than the price shown.

This is the market the model is actually good at. Scored over 24,126 pairs across seven championships, it beats a season-best ranking on Brier by 0.0057 (95% bootstrap 0.0011–0.0098) — an edge it does not have when picking outright winners. Probabilities near 50/50 are honest: quoted 0.552, realised 0.560. But treat lopsided pairs with care even after recalibration — the model still runs slightly hot at the top of the range, and a short matchup price is where that costs you.

Ranked by predicted finishing position, not by win probability — they are not the same order, and they disagree in the top four of 28 of the 44 events. Avg pos is the athlete's mean finishing position across 50,000 simulated finals. A volatile athlete can hold the better win chance while finishing worse on average, because the tail that wins races is the same tail that produces sixth places.

Fair odds, not offered odds. These are the model's own prices. Back a runner only where a book is offering longer than the Fair column, and de-vig the book's full market before you compare — athletics outrights routinely carry a 130%+ overround.
